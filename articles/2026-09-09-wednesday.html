The Buyback Stops Working

Three weeks ago, the Treasury's bond buybacks calmed the long end for a day. On Wednesday it tripled them — and yields jumped to their highest since 2023 anyway. With oil back over $100 and the Fed a week out, the market's crutch just gave way.

Hunter William LangMarkets — Momentum Check
Wed · Sep 9, 2026

There is a difference between a market that is panicking and one that is bracing, and Wednesday was the latter. Stocks fell for a third straight session — the S&P 500 down 0.48% to 7,636.36, the Dow off 405 points to 52,380.66 — as two familiar pressures tightened at once: oil back above $100 and Treasury yields climbing to levels not seen in years. The unsettling part wasn't the decline. It was what stopped working.

The crutch gives way

Rewind three weeks. In August, when the long bond was in revolt, the Treasury doubled its buybacks of long-dated debt and yields fell for a day — a small, engineered relief. On Wednesday it went further, announcing it would triple the next buyback program to $6 billion. The market's response was the opposite of relief: the 10-year yield rose to 4.857%, its highest since 2023. Investors read the escalation not as support but as an admission — that the long-end problem is getting worse, not better, and that a bigger bid from Washington is a symptom, not a cure. The same medicine, three times the dose, and the patient got sicker.

Crude did its part. Brent settled above $100 a barrel for the first time since July as the conflict with Iran escalated again, extending a run that has now lifted oil for the better part of two weeks. Energy was the tape's only real refuge — Chevron, Occidental and Exxon among the few green names — while the megacaps that lead in calmer weather did the damage: Alphabet fell more than 3%, Salesforce about 3%, and small caps, always the most allergic to high rates, dropped over 1%. A rebound in Intel and Oracle, each up around 5% on company-specific news, was a footnote.

That is the vise. Two inflation reports land Thursday and Friday, the last the Fed will see before it meets next week, and they arrive with crude freshly over $100 — a live upside risk to both prints. A market that spent the summer arguing the Fed would stay patient is now staring at a hike, higher energy, and a long end that won't behave no matter what Washington throws at it. Sentiment has turned accordingly: the widely watched Fear & Greed gauge has slipped into “fear” from neutral a week ago.

The momentum read

Decisively defensive. Three down days, breadth narrowed to energy alone, and the Treasury's buyback tool losing its potency — which means the long end is now its own risk, separate from whatever the Fed decides. Watch Friday's CPI and whether Brent holds $100. Until both cool, rallies are for selling, not chasing.
