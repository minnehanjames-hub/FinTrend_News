The Inflation Test

Last week the labor market handed the bulls a reason to believe the Fed can afford to wait. This week inflation gets a vote — and Wednesday's CPI report will decide whether the “patient Fed” trade has legs or borrowed time.

James Thomas MinnehanMarkets — The Monday Outlook
Mon · Aug 10, 2026

The S&P 500 begins the week at a record. It got there on Friday for an unlikely reason — a jobs report showing employers cut 23,000 positions in July — on the logic that a cooling labor market gives Kevin Warsh's Fed room to hold rates rather than raise them. The 10-year yield fell to 4.64%, and the index closed at 7,757.64. That is the setup. The test comes Wednesday.

Why Wednesday is the whole week

A weak payrolls print only helps stocks if inflation cooperates. If July's CPI runs hot, Warsh — who has been unusually blunt about keeping high inflation from becoming entrenched — gets fresh ammunition for the hawks, and the market's tidy “the Fed can wait” narrative gets messy in a hurry. If it comes in tame, the patient-Fed trade extends and the record can hold.

Futures traders enter the week roughly split, pricing about even odds that the Fed simply holds in September. That is a market with no conviction — which is exactly why a single data point can move it so far. Fed governor Lisa Cook, who favored holding in July, has said plainly she is prepared to act if she doesn't see inflation coming down. Wednesday tells her, and everyone else, which way to lean.

A soft jobs number and a hot inflation number is the one combination this rally cannot digest. We find out Wednesday whether we have it.The Monday Outlook

What we're watching underneath

Beyond the print: the consumer. Friday's retail sales and sentiment data will show whether the weakness in hiring is bleeding into spending. And oil remains the silent variable — a genuine move to reopen the Strait of Hormuz would cool inflation and take pressure off the long end at once, while a breakdown in talks would do the opposite. Records are a fine place to start a week. Whether we end it there depends on numbers we don't yet have.

The outlook

Constructive but contingent. The path of least resistance is higher only if Wednesday's CPI validates the bet that a softer labor market lets the Fed hold. Watch the print, watch the consumer on Friday, and watch oil — any one of them can rewrite the week.
