Friday Recap · Week of Oct 5

A Record, Then a Reality Check on AI

The S&P set its first record since August on Tuesday and finished the week up 1.2% at 7,811.54. In between, the Fed’s minutes kept a year-end hike on the table, the 10-year touched 5.36%, and a report that OpenAI’s revenue is running about $20 billion below what had circulated gave the AI trade its roughest day in weeks.

HWL
Hunter William Lang
Markets Editor
FRI · OCT 9 · 2026

A week ago the question was whether earnings could take over from the Fed as the market’s driver. By Tuesday the market had its answer, at least for a day: the S&P 500 closed at a record 7,818.93, its first all-time high since August, and the Nasdaq set records on Monday and Tuesday on a wave of takeovers and an AI power deal. Then the week got harder. Wednesday’s Fed minutes showed most officials still expect another hike by year-end, and the 10-year Treasury touched 5.36%, its highest since 2002. On Thursday, a Financial Times report that OpenAI’s annualized revenue is running about $20 billion below the figure that had circulated knocked 1.3% off the Nasdaq.

Friday put most of it back. The S&P rose 0.6% to 7,811.54, finishing the week up 1.2% and less than 0.1% below Tuesday’s record. The Dow gained 0.8% on the day and 0.9% on the week, and the Nasdaq ended up 0.6%. The 10-year settled near 5.24%, well off Wednesday’s peak. The exception was the same one this column flagged on Wednesday: the Russell 2000 fell 0.9% for the week, the only major index to finish lower.

The week, day by day

Two record days built on deals, two down days built on the Fed and on doubts about AI, and a Friday rebound that left the scoreboard looking calmer than the week felt. The split between the indexes says where the money went: into the largest companies and defensive sectors, and out of small caps.

Wednesday: the minutes make the case for one more

The minutes of the Sept. 15–16 meeting were as hawkish as the market feared, which is partly why they did little damage. All 19 participants backed September’s quarter-point hike to 3.75%–4.00%, and most said another increase would likely be appropriate by year-end. Officials pointed to three sources of price pressure: higher oil and fuel costs from the Middle East conflict, AI-related spending, and the lingering effect of tariffs. Several judged that policy had not been restrictive, or only mildly so, before the hike. And some worried that after more than five years of inflation above 2%, high inflation could start to feel normal.

The bond market tested that message before it accepted it. The 10-year touched 5.36% during the session — above last week’s 5.34% and the highest since 2002 — before strong demand at a Treasury auction pulled it back toward 5.28%. Futures kept the odds of an October hike at roughly 20% to 25%, while still pricing a move by year-end. The S&P slipped 0.2% from its record.

Thursday: the AI trade gets audited

The FT reported that OpenAI’s annualized revenue was about $50 billion at the end of September, against the roughly $70 billion that had been circulating. For a market whose leaders are priced on the assumption that AI demand is compounding, a $20 billion gap in the industry’s flagship customer was a reason to take profits. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell as much as 4% before paring losses, Nvidia and Micron slid, and Microsoft and Oracle fell with them. The Nasdaq closed down 1.25% at 27,193 and the S&P lost 0.5%, but the Dow eked out a 0.1% gain as energy and consumer staples rose.

Oil supplied the other half of the day. Brent jumped $4.08 to settle at $104.28 after fresh tanker attacks in the Gulf, reports that Washington was weighing strikes on Iran, and production shut-ins ahead of Hurricane Isaias. Prices eased only after President Trump said the U.S. would not attack Iran before the Nov. 3 midterms. PepsiCo, meanwhile, offered a preview of how consumer companies are handling the shock: it beat forecasts with adjusted earnings of $2.34 a share on $25.27 billion of revenue, then cut its 2026 core profit growth outlook to 2.5%–3.5% while raising its sales forecast. Volumes are holding up. Margins are not.

Friday: fuel bills and a telecom shock

Delta made the energy shock concrete. The airline missed estimates for the first time in two years, with adjusted earnings of $1.72 a share against $1.76 expected and revenue of $17.59 billion. Its fuel bill rose 62% from a year earlier to $4.14 billion, as the price it paid per gallon climbed 60% to $3.61. Delta cut its 2026 earnings forecast to $5.10–$5.60 a share from $6.50–$7.50, citing about $6 billion of extra fuel costs, even as chief executive Ed Bastian insisted demand remains strong. The shares fell in early trading.

The day’s biggest moves came from an unexpected direction. SpaceX agreed to buy a nationwide block of low-band wireless spectrum, and investors read it as a direct challenge to the carriers: T-Mobile, AT&T and Verizon each fell roughly 10% or more by midday, while tower owner Crown Castle jumped about 11%. Communication services was the worst sector in the S&P, down 1.6%. Health care and real estate led instead, helped by a 12% jump in Humana after regulators restored a four-star rating to its flagship Medicare Advantage plan.

The data was gloomy. The University of Michigan’s preliminary October sentiment index fell to 46.3 from 48.1, below the 47.8 forecasters expected, and its gauge of current conditions dropped to 44.7. One-year inflation expectations rose to 4.7% and the five-to-ten-year measure to 3.5%. Brent edged up to settle at $104.72 as Isaias, now a major hurricane, forced producers to shut in more than 60% of U.S. Gulf oil output before landfall Friday night. Trump called talks with Tehran “productive” but said the blockade stays in place, and Iran’s foreign minister said Tehran was reviewing the U.S. response to its proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

A market that set a record, absorbed a hawkish Fed, a 5.36% 10-year and a first crack in the AI revenue story, and still finished up 1.2% is a sturdy market. A Russell 2000 that fell on the week says it is still a narrow one.FinTrend Markets Desk

The bottom line

This was a good week on the scoreboard and an informative one underneath. The record proved the leaders can still pull the index higher with the long end above 5.2%. Thursday proved how sensitive those leaders are to any sign that AI revenue is growing more slowly than the spending behind it. And Delta and PepsiCo showed that the cost of a $100-plus oil price is now landing in corporate guidance, not just in consumer surveys. Next week the bond market is closed Monday for Columbus Day; JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo report Tuesday; September CPI lands Wednesday alongside Bank of America and Morgan Stanley; and retail sales, wholesale prices and TSMC follow Thursday.

The bottom line

The S&P rose 1.2% to 7,811.54, a whisker below Tuesday’s record, with the Dow up 0.9% and the Nasdaq 0.6%. Most Fed officials still see one more hike by year-end, the 10-year touched 5.36% before settling near 5.24%, and a report that OpenAI’s revenue run-rate is about $50 billion — not $70 billion — gave the AI trade its first real audit. Delta’s $6 billion fuel hit and PepsiCo’s profit cut put the oil shock into guidance. Small caps fell 0.9%. Next up: the big banks and September CPI.
