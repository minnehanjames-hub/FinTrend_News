Momentum Check: The Rebound Waits on Meta

Three straight gains have put the S&P 500 back within 0.8% of its record, led by a blowout quarter from SpaceX and a broad bounce in small caps. But the 10-year is back at 5.12%, the 20-year auction tailed, and Meta reports tonight with the AI financing question still open.

HWL
Hunter William Lang
Markets Editor
WED · OCT 21 · 2026

Last week ended with the Nasdaq’s worst week since June and a question nobody could answer: who pays for the AI build-out when the 10-year Treasury yields 5%? Three sessions later, the market has decided to buy first and ask later. The S&P 500 rose 0.6% on Wednesday to 7,757.17, its third straight gain, and is now up 1.3% for the week and within 0.8% of its Oct. 6 record close of 7,818.93. The Nasdaq added 1.1% to 26,702.86, the Dow 0.7% to 52,856.09 and the Russell 2000 0.3% to 2,781.02.

The leadership tells you what changed and what didn’t. This was not a bond rally: the 10-year yield climbed back to 5.14% on Monday and finished Wednesday at 5.12%, above where it started the week. It was an earnings rally. SpaceX, which controls xAI, rose 7.8% after a quarter that beat every estimate, and Nvidia’s 2.5% gain was the single biggest lift to the S&P. Tonight Meta, Tesla, IBM, Lam Research and Texas Instruments report after the close, and they will test whether that confidence was earned.

Monday was narrow. Nvidia and the other AI names bounced, but most U.S. stocks outside technology fell and the Dow closed slightly lower. Tuesday was the opposite: the Nasdaq was flat as Apple and Meta slipped, while the Dow rose 530 points and the Russell 2000 gained 1.7% on strong reports from Equifax, Hasbro and 3M. Airlines rallied as Brent fell back from the morning’s highs. Wednesday put the two halves together: AI stocks and the Dow rose on the same day for the first time this week.

SpaceX did the heavy lifting

SpaceX reported third-quarter revenue of about $14.6 billion, up 161% from a year earlier and well above the roughly $12.9 billion analysts expected. Net income of about $4.4 billion was more than double the forecast. The AI business, which includes xAI, brought in about $7.6 billion, more than half of total revenue and about $1.3 billion above estimates. Elon Musk also said SpaceX had taken a step toward considering a possible merger with Tesla, which helped lift Tesla 3.4% ahead of its own report tonight. Super Micro Computer rose 9.5% after raising its revenue outlook on demand for AI systems.

The detail that matters for this market is on the spending line. SpaceX’s capital spending was about $19.6 billion in the quarter, more than its revenue. Investors were happy to look past that because the AI revenue arrived faster than expected. That is the bargain the market is offering the AI spenders right now: show growth that outruns the forecasts, and the capex is forgiven. The harder question, how that capex is financed, did not come up on Wednesday. It will tonight.

The misses that mattered were on guidance

The stocks that fell this week did not miss on the quarter; they missed on the outlook. Netflix earned $0.87 a share on revenue of $12.87 billion, up about 12% and in line with forecasts, but guided fourth-quarter revenue to $13.44 billion, below expectations, landed at the low end of its full-year range at $51.1 billion and cut its advertising revenue target to about $2.7 billion from about $3 billion. The shares fell 5.3% on Wednesday, extending their run of losses after earnings reports. AT&T fell 6.4% on a growth forecast that disappointed, and General Motors lost 6.8% on Tuesday even after a profit beat and a buyback of up to $20 billion, because the midpoint of its 2026 forecast came in below estimates.

Capital One, by contrast, rose 2.5% after beating forecasts, another sign that consumer credit is holding up even as sentiment surveys sit near record lows. With FactSet showing analysts expecting about 29% earnings growth for the S&P 500 this quarter, the bar for the quarter itself is high, but the market is now grading companies on next year.

Breadth: the small caps are pulling their weight

The most encouraging part of the rebound is that it is not just Nvidia. The Russell 2000 is up 2.2% for the week, matching the Nasdaq and outpacing the S&P 500’s 1.3%, and the Dow has gained 1.6%. After a week in which semiconductors fell more than 11% and the rest of the market held up, money is now moving into both groups at once. That is healthier than the narrow rallies of September, when new lows kept outnumbering new highs.

The warning sign is volatility at the edge of the AI trade. CoreWeave, which lost a quarter of its value last week, swung between a gain of nearly 4% and a loss of 4.5% on Wednesday before finishing down 0.5%; on Tuesday it went from down 6% to up 5%. South Korea’s Kospi, dominated by Samsung and SK Hynix, fell 3.7% on Tuesday and rose 3.3% on Wednesday. Prices that move this much in a day have not found a level, and neither has the AI credit chain behind them.

The bond market isn’t celebrating

Last week’s drop in yields was a flight from the AI trade, not a verdict on inflation, and this week’s auctions are testing how much of that bid was real. The Treasury sold $13 billion of 20-year bonds on Wednesday at 5.469%, about 0.9 basis point above where the bond was trading before the sale, against an average tail of 0.2 basis point. Overall demand was solid, with a bid-to-cover ratio of 2.73 against a 2.62 average, but indirect bidders, a proxy for foreign buyers, took 58.2% against a 65.2% average. The long end still needs a price concession to clear.

Europe is adding to the pressure. France’s 10-year yield briefly touched 5% on Wednesday for the first time since 2002, after riots over living costs and fuel prices over the weekend and as Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu’s minority government seeks €54 billion of savings in its 2027 budget. France plans to borrow a record €340 billion next year. When a major euro-area government pays 5% to borrow, the global supply of high-quality duration looks less scarce, and that keeps a floor under U.S. yields. Thursday’s $26 billion five-year TIPS auction is the next test.

Oil is calmer, not solved

Brent came close to $112 on Tuesday morning, its highest level since the war with Iran began in late February, before falling back to $108.79; it settled at $108.82 on Wednesday. The Monday dip followed President Trump’s pause of a package of Russia sanctions, which is likely to keep more Russian energy flowing. In the Gulf nothing has changed: attacks on shipping near Hormuz have not stopped, and Tehran’s conditions for reopening the strait still stand. Oil has traded between $97 and $112 this month, and each spike toward the top of that range has been met with selling. That pattern holds only as long as there is no new disruption.

Tonight and the rest of the week

Meta has guided third-quarter revenue to $61 billion to $64 billion, with consensus near $63.3 billion, and 2026 capital spending to $130 billion to $145 billion. The quarter matters less than two other things: whether spending is still covered by operating cash flow, and what Meta says about 2027. Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles in the quarter and needs to show margins held up. Lam Research and Texas Instruments will show whether chip-equipment and analog demand survived last week’s rout in semiconductors, and IBM gives a read on enterprise software.

Thursday brings Procter & Gamble, Blackstone, Union Pacific and Lockheed Martin, weekly jobless claims and the TIPS auction. Friday brings American Express and SLB, S&P Global’s flash PMIs for October and the final University of Michigan survey, where the preliminary reading showed long-run inflation expectations at 3.5%. The rebound needs Meta to show discipline on spending, or at least cash to pay for it. If it does, the record is within reach this week. If it doesn’t, expect last week’s question to return quickly.

The momentum read

The S&P 500 rose for a third straight day to 7,757.17, up 1.3% for the week and 0.8% below its record, as SpaceX’s blowout quarter revived the AI trade and small caps gained 2.2%. The rebound has come without help from bonds: the 10-year is back at 5.12%, Wednesday’s 20-year auction tailed and France’s 10-year touched 5%. Guidance misses at Netflix, GM and AT&T show the market is grading next year, not this quarter. Meta, Tesla, Lam Research and Texas Instruments report tonight; Meta’s spending and how it is funded will decide whether the record falls this week.
