Two Referees, One Week

Wednesday night, Nvidia rules on whether the AI capex engine is still roaring. Friday morning, Kevin Warsh rules on whether the Fed is about to hike. Rarely do the two forces holding up this market get judged in the same five days.

James Thomas MinnehanMarkets — The Monday Outlook
Mon · Aug 24, 2026

This market rests on two pillars: an artificial-intelligence build-out that keeps corporate spending booming, and a Federal Reserve that traders still hope will hold rather than hike. This week, both get tested — by name. The S&P 500 opened the week slipping 0.3% to 7,652.86, drifting off its early-August record as investors positioned for the two events that matter more than any tape between now and September.

Why both, why now

Nvidia is no longer just a chipmaker — it is the market's proxy for whether the AI spending cycle that has carried 2026 is still intensifying or beginning to mature. A strong outlook re-arms the bulls; any hint of a plateau lands on the single most crowded trade on Wall Street. Then, forty-eight hours later, Warsh takes the stage in Wyoming with the bond market in revolt, inflation sticky, and a chorus of officials openly favoring higher rates. Monday's dip was the market clearing its throat before both.

One speech and one earnings call. Between them they will price the two things this rally cannot live without: cheap money and endless AI demand.The Monday Outlook

The set-up underneath

Oil eased Monday and yields came in with it, a small reprieve for a bond market that spent last week at multi-decade highs. But that calm is conditional. A hot PCE print Wednesday would hand Warsh cover to sound hawkish Friday, and a cautious Nvidia outlook would remove the market's best argument that the AI boom justifies its valuations. The path higher needs both pillars to hold. This is the week we find out if they will.

The outlook

A binary, event-driven week. Watch Wednesday's PCE and Nvidia's guidance, then Warsh on Friday. If inflation runs warm and Warsh leans hawkish, the September hike debate goes live — and even a blockbuster Nvidia print may not be enough to keep records in view.
