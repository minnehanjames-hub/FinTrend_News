Momentum Check: Narrowing Into the Decision

Hours before the Fed’s call, the market’s internals are flashing caution — leadership has thinned, the chips have broken down, and breadth is quietly deteriorating beneath a headline index that looks calmer than it is.

HWL
Hunter William Lang
Markets Editor
WED · SEP 16 · 2026

In a few hours the Federal Reserve will almost certainly raise interest rates for the first time in three years, and the commentary will focus entirely on that. But momentum — the actual force under the tape — had already made its statement before the FOMC said a word. Over the past two sessions the market has quietly narrowed: a shrinking group of stocks doing the lifting, a broken chip complex, and a headline S&P that masks how much erosion is happening beneath it.

That is the job of a momentum check — to look past the index print at whether the market is being carried by many stocks or a few, and whether the leaders are still leading. Right now the answer is uncomfortable, and it sets a fragile stage for whatever Chair Warsh delivers this afternoon.

Leadership has thinned

The clearest momentum signal is what happened to the generals. Semiconductors led this bull market on the way up; this week they led on the way down. Monday’s AI-safety scare took the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down nearly 6% — its worst session since July — and the group has not repaired the damage since. When the strongest trend in the market breaks its footing, momentum investors take notice regardless of what the Fed does, because it removes the engine that powered every prior rally.

One green Friday, then two red days that erased it. That is the definition of failed momentum — a rally that could not extend past a single session. The dip that would normally be bought is instead being sold, and the absence of buyers on Tuesday is the more telling half of the story.

Breadth is worse than the index

Here is the sleight of hand in this week’s tape: the Nasdaq has held up better than the chips inside it because money rotated into cybersecurity and software rather than leaving the market entirely. That is genuinely constructive — a rotation has a floor a liquidation does not. But it also means the index is being propped by a defensive huddle, not by broad participation. The Russell 2000, the purest read on breadth and rate sensitivity, has been the weakest major all week. When small caps lag this consistently, momentum is narrowing whatever the headline says.

A single green day surrounded by red is not a trend reversal — it is a market testing whether anyone still wants to buy the dip. This week, twice, the answer was no.FinTrend Momentum Desk

What good momentum would look like after 2 p.m.

Because the decision lands mid-session, the honest momentum call is conditional. A constructive outcome would be the index reclaiming ground on expanding breadth — small caps and semis participating, not just software holding the line. A poor outcome would be a knee-jerk pop that fades into the close on narrow participation, the classic “sell-the-relief” pattern. The level to respect on the S&P is its 50-day moving average; the chips have already lost theirs, and if the broad index follows, the technical picture turns from “pullback” to something that needs watching.

The momentum read

Beneath a composed-looking index, momentum has narrowed hard into the Fed decision: chip leadership is broken, breadth is thin, and buyers went missing on two straight down days. The one thing holding it together is a rotation into cyber and software — a floor, not an engine. Watch the reaction after 2 p.m. for breadth, not just the print: a bounce that fades on narrow participation would confirm the loss of momentum; a broad reclaim would argue the pullback has run its course.
