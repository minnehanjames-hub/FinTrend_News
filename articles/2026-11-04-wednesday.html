Momentum Check: The Election Rally Left the Chips Behind

The Dow jumped 1,569 points and the S&P 500 had its best day in three months after Democrats won control of Congress and Brent fell 8.7% below $95. But Nvidia fell 3.1% and CoreWeave 9.9% after Texas elected a governor who campaigned against data center projects.

HWL
Hunter William Lang
Markets Editor
WED · NOV 4 · 2026

The market got the election result it could most easily price, and it spent Wednesday pricing two very different things. The Dow jumped 1,569.40 points, or 3.0%, to 53,356.12, and the S&P 500 rose 1.6% to 7,688.62, its best day in three months, after Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress and Brent crude fell 8.7% to $94.62. The S&P is now 1.4% higher for the week and 1.7% below its record. But the rally did not reach the stocks that carry the AI build-out. Nvidia fell 3.1% and was the heaviest weight on the index, and CoreWeave lost 9.9%, after Texas elected a governor who campaigned against data center projects.

Monday was a rotation day. The S&P 500 opened up 1.1% and closed down 0.5% as AI stocks gave back an early bounce, but most stocks in the index rose, and nearly seven in ten stocks outside AI and tech gained. Brent fell 7.6% on hopes for Iran mediation, and the companies that burn fuel led: United Airlines rose 4.8%, Carnival 4.2% and Marriott 4.3% on stronger hotel trends. Marvell fell 12.7% after agreeing to buy Lumentum for 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share, and Lumentum dropped 18.6%. The ISM manufacturing index came in stronger than expected at 55.5, and its prices gauge eased to 75.2 from 77.9.

Tuesday was the mirror image: the S&P rose 0.4% even though most of its members fell, because Nvidia, Broadcom, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta all gained. Apple fell 1.9% after beating forecasts but guiding to lower margins in the holiday quarter as AI-driven demand raises memory chip costs. Palantir slipped 1.6% despite reporting 148% growth in U.S. commercial revenue. More job openings than expected pushed the 10-year yield up to 4.96%.

Wednesday: the result and the reaction

By Wednesday morning Democrats had won both the House and the Senate. Early in the day the AP and New York Times counts gave Democrats 228 House seats with several races uncalled, a gain of 19, and at least 54 Senate seats, with seven Republican seats flipped and Alaska and Mississippi still undecided. In Texas, Democrat Gina Hinojosa defeated Gov. Greg Abbott. President Trump said he retains full veto power, which means the new Congress can block his agenda more easily than it can pass its own. Deutsche Bank’s Jim Reid described the outcome as a normal midterm by historical standards, and RBC’s Jeremy Richardson said it matched what markets expected. Brian Jacobsen of Annex Wealth Management summed up the session in three words: “Earnings trump elections.”

The biggest market move was in oil. Vice President JD Vance attributed part of the Republican losses to the war with Iran, and traders read that as pressure to scale the war back. Brent fell 8.7% to $94.62, its lowest level in two months, and is now down about 17% since Friday. GasBuddy’s Patrick De Haan said the national average for gasoline could fall below $4 a gallon if prices hold. Cheaper oil also helped bonds: the 10-year yield fell to 4.88% from 4.95% on election night.

The winners and losers were political

Sector moves read like a policy map. Defense contractors fell, with Lockheed Martin down 6.9%, Northrop Grumman 4.5% and General Dynamics 2.6%. Oil producers dropped with crude: Exxon Mobil lost 4.6% and ConocoPhillips 5.2%. Hospital operator HCA rose 10.3%, Dollar Tree 9.6%, and solar names First Solar and Enphase gained 4.6% and 3.7%. Trump Media & Technology Group fell 24.5%, Tesla 3.3% and SpaceX 3.8%. Overseas, Paris rose about 2%, London 1.8% and Shanghai 2.2%; Tokyo fell 1.4%.

Why the chips sat out

The AI leaders did not join the rally, and the reason was on the ballot. Electricity bills and the cost of living became campaign issues across party lines, and data center construction became a local fight in several states. Texas, where Hinojosa ran against data center projects, is home to a large share of planned U.S. capacity. Voters in an Ohio county approved a ban on large data centers where Meta had planned a facility. Analyst Dan Ives told ABC News the Senate result would create major hurdles for AI and tech legislation. Nvidia fell 3.1% and CoreWeave 9.9%. Alphabet, which had its worst day on record last Thursday, rose 3.2%.

The market liked divided government and cheaper oil. It did not like the idea that a data center now needs a permit from voters.Hunter William Lang

The tension is now plain. A Democratic Congress cannot pass much over a presidential veto, and that is the main reason the broad market rallied. But data center permits are mostly decided by states, counties and utility regulators, not by Congress, and that is where the election changed the most. For the companies whose growth depends on building more capacity faster, the relevant map is not Washington. It is Austin, Columbus and a few hundred county boards.

Tonight and the rest of the week

AppLovin reports after the close. On Thursday the Bank of England is expected to raise its rate for the first time since 2023, and productivity and jobless claims come out at 8:30 a.m. Friday brings the October jobs report, with economists expecting about 59,000 new jobs and a 4.2% unemployment rate after September’s 29,000. Wednesday’s ISM services survey showed activity still expanding at 54.5, slightly below forecasts, with its prices index easing to 73.0. A strong payroll number would keep the December hike case alive even with oil below $95.

The momentum read

The S&P 500 rose 1.6% to 7,688.62, its best day in three months, and the Dow jumped 3.0% after Democrats won control of Congress and Brent fell 8.7% below $95 on expectations of pressure to scale back the war with Iran. The S&P is up 1.4% for the week and 1.7% below its record. But Nvidia fell 3.1% and CoreWeave 9.9% after Texas elected a governor who campaigned against data center projects. Oil, defense and AI infrastructure lost; hospitals, discount retail and travel won. The Bank of England and Friday’s jobs report come next.
