The Last Word Before the Fed

Markets are dark for Labor Day, but the quiet is a setup, not a rest. This holiday-shortened week delivers the last two inflation readings the Fed will see before it decides — and after Friday's hot jobs report and a weekend of fresh Iran strikes, a rate hike is no longer the tail risk. It's the base case.

James Thomas MinnehanMarkets — The Monday Outlook
Mon · Sep 7, 2026

There is no opening bell on Monday — U.S. markets are closed for Labor Day — but this may be the most consequential quiet week of the autumn. Over the next four sessions the government releases the final two inflation reports the Federal Reserve will see before it meets next week, and it does so with Wall Street already braced for something it hasn't done all cycle: raise interest rates. Coming off Friday's blowout jobs report and a weekend of renewed strikes between the U.S. and Iran, the setup could hardly be tenser.

Why these two prints matter more than usual

The arithmetic changed on Friday. August payrolls came in at 162,000 — roughly triple expectations — and the prior two months were revised higher, erasing the July weakness that had let the market dream of a patient Fed. Layered on Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole keynote, that report pushed the odds of a September hike to about 58%, with some measures higher; Deutsche Bank now calls a hike the “most likely” outcome of the meeting. In that light, Thursday's PPI and Friday's CPI are not routine data. They are the last chance for inflation to talk the Fed out of a move. A hot print seals it; only a clearly soft one reopens the debate.

The oil problem won't wait

The wildcard is energy, and it is moving the wrong way. The U.S. and Iran traded fresh strikes over the weekend, and crude has climbed back toward $100 a barrel, with Brent near $97 and rising. Tehran has threatened to establish a maritime exclusion zone across the Persian Gulf in response to what it calls U.S. “economic warfare.” Higher energy feeds directly into the very inflation figures landing Thursday and Friday — and into the Fed's calculus a week later. It is the one variable neither the data nor the central bank controls, and right now it is pulling toward a hike.

The Fed spent the year waiting for permission to cut. This week's data may instead hand it a reason to hike.The Monday Outlook

The setup

Treat this as a two-act week. The first act is the data — inflation Thursday and Friday. The second is the Fed, the week after. With a hike now the base case, the burden of proof has flipped: it is on the numbers to argue the Fed out of moving, not the other way around. Watch the 2-year yield, at 4.37% its highest since January 2025, and watch Brent. If both keep climbing through the prints, next week's decision will be all but written before the committee sits down.

The outlook

A deceptively quiet holiday open before the year's most consequential data-and-Fed stretch. PPI Thursday and CPI Friday are the last word before the decision, and oil is the swing factor. With the hike case hardening, the risk-reward favors caution into the prints — let the data set the tone rather than front-running it.
