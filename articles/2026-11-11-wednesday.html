Momentum Check: CPI Settled December. CoreWeave Steadied the Chips.

October CPI ran hot and futures fully priced a December hike, sending the two-year yield up 19 basis points. Then CoreWeave beat forecasts and promised about $9 billion of customer prepayments, and by late morning Wednesday the S&P 500 was up 1.1% and the Nasdaq 1.8%, led by chips.

HWL
Hunter William Lang
Markets Editor
WED · NOV 11 · 2026

Hot inflation on Tuesday, a better funding story on Wednesday. October consumer prices rose 0.4% and core prices 0.3% for a third straight month, and by Tuesday’s close futures had fully priced a Fed hike on Dec. 9, with the odds of a half-point move near 12%. The two-year Treasury yield jumped 19 basis points, Nvidia fell another 5.4% as its board investigates its chief executive, and the S&P 500 slipped 0.4%. Then CoreWeave, the most closely watched of the debt-funded cloud builders, beat forecasts, raised its outlook and said customers will prepay about $9 billion this quarter. As of about 11 a.m. ET on Wednesday, the S&P 500 was up 1.1% at 7,603.10, the Nasdaq 1.8% and the Russell 2000 0.6%, with chip stocks in front; the Dow was slightly lower. The bond market is closed for Veterans Day. Wednesday’s figures here are intraday and will move by the close.

Monday was a relief rally built on oil. President Trump told CNN that renewed strikes on Iran were highly unlikely if negotiations progress, Brent fell 5.8% to $92.57, and the S&P 500 had its best day since April. Chip stocks led the bounce after falling more than 12% the week before: AMD rose 12.9%, Broadcom 8.8% and the Philadelphia semiconductor index nearly 7%. Morgan Stanley and UBS told clients that last week’s selloff had overshot the real risk from data center restrictions. Nvidia was the exception, falling 4.3% to its lowest close since April.

Tuesday: the CPI did what the Fed feared

Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% economists expected and the third straight monthly gain of 0.4% or more. Core prices, which exclude food and energy, rose 0.3%, also above forecasts and the third 0.3% reading in a row. Energy rose 0.7% and gasoline 1.0%, food 0.5%, shelter 0.3% and motor vehicle insurance 0.7%; used cars fell 1.2%. The Labor Department did not publish year-over-year rates, because it collected no prices in October 2025 during the government shutdown, so the monthly figures are the whole story. Joseph Brusuelas of RSM said higher oil, gasoline and diesel prices are still passing through to transportation services and groceries even though crude has fallen, and that the data “demands a December rate hike.”

The Fed did not wait for the number to speak. Governor Christopher Waller said in London, hours before the release, that the Fed should raise rates in December even if inflation cooled and should consider a half-point move if it did not; core at 0.3% met his test. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said the Fed needs at least two more quarter-point hikes, and Kansas City Fed President Jeff Schmid called current policy “fairly accommodative.” By the close, CME’s FedWatch showed a December hike fully priced, with the odds of a half-point move at about 12%, up from zero a day earlier. The two-year Treasury yield jumped 18.9 basis points to 4.74%, its biggest daily rise since April 2025, and the 10-year rose 12.6 basis points to 4.99%.

Stocks took the news calmly at the index level. The S&P 500 lost 0.4% and the Nasdaq 0.7%, and technology was the weakest of the 11 sectors, down 1.4%. Jason Ware of Albion Financial Group called the selling “irrational,” arguing that the inflation is driven by energy and should ease if oil keeps falling. That argument is right about the source and early on the timing: October’s prices reflect crude that was well above today’s level for most of the month, and the Fed decides on Dec. 9, a day before November’s CPI is released.

Nvidia: a governance question in the index’s biggest stock

Nvidia fell 5.4% on Tuesday, bringing its two-day loss to 9.6%, as its board pressed ahead with an independent investigation of chief executive Jensen Huang’s relationship with a business executive whom U.S. intelligence reportedly concluded was gathering information for China. The board has hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz and has said it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. Wedbush analysts said Huang’s removal would be “the worst possible outcome” for the company. On Wednesday, Baird cut the stock to Neutral, citing leadership uncertainty and limited visibility into 2027 data center demand, and Morgan Stanley removed it from its top-pick list under a stop-loss rule while keeping an Overweight rating.

The investigation lands a week before Nvidia reports its third-quarter results on Nov. 17. For the market, the issue is less about the quarter than about key-person risk at one of the largest weights in the S&P 500. Whatever the board concludes, the stock now trades on governance news as well as on orders, and that is a new kind of volatility for the index’s anchor.

CoreWeave: the funding answer the market wanted

CoreWeave reported Tuesday evening, and it addressed the exact worry that sank AI infrastructure stocks last week. Revenue rose 168% to $3.66 billion, ahead of the $3.54 billion consensus. Backlog reached $132.5 billion, up from $104 billion three months earlier. The company raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion, above the $12.86 billion analysts expected, and guided fourth-quarter revenue to $4.7 billion to $5.0 billion. It expects to spend $37 billion to $39 billion this year to bring capacity online.

The balance sheet is still heavy. CoreWeave lost $709 million in the quarter, total debt reached $47 billion and interest expense was $846 million, against $310 million a year earlier. But management said it expects about $9 billion of customer prepayments and deposits in the fourth quarter and will not tap the unsecured bond market at current interest rates, a direct answer to last week’s record credit default swap spreads and bond yields above 16%. Chief executive Michael Intrator said he does not expect post-election regulatory changes to materially affect the company’s expansion pipeline. The stock had fallen more than 40% in the month before the report.

Wednesday morning: chips first, the Dow lags

The rebound is concentrated in the stocks that fell hardest last week. In morning trading CoreWeave was up more than 10%, Nvidia about 4%, and Micron and Broadcom about 5.6% each, according to the AP. GE Vernova, which sells power equipment to data centers, rose 3%, and Nebius, which reports on Thursday, jumped about 15% early. Fossil soared about 31% after a stronger quarter, while StubHub fell 7%. The Dow, which holds fewer AI names, was slightly lower, and the AP noted that more S&P 500 stocks were falling than rising. That is the reverse of last week, when most stocks held up while AI sank.

Oil is the counterweight. Brent rose about 1.3% to around $92.70 after a senior Iranian security official said Tehran will not allow enriched nuclear material to leave the country, and Iran’s Revolutionary Guards navy said two tankers caught fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz, Reuters reported. Washington wants nuclear issues included in any initial talks. Giovanni Staunovo of UBS said prices are being supported by fading hopes of an imminent de-escalation. Brent is still down about 21% since the start of November. With Treasurys shut for the holiday, the bond market’s response to Wednesday’s moves arrives Thursday morning. One more data point on households: the CNBC/NRF Retail Monitor showed October retail sales up just 0.2%, the slowest pace since November 2025.

Anthropic sets its price range

Anthropic, which plans to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, set a range of $70 to $78 a share for 825 million Class A shares. At the top of the range it would raise about $64.4 billion at a fully diluted valuation of roughly $1.4 trillion, and trading could begin as soon as next week. Avery Marquez of Renaissance Capital said he does not expect demand to be in question. Reuters noted that investors expect the listing to compete with other AI heavyweights for the same dollars. A deal that size, priced in the same weeks as a December hike and an Nvidia governance review, is the clearest test yet of how much new AI paper the market can absorb.

The rest of the week

The bond market reopens on Thursday, when it will price Wednesday’s oil and equity moves for the first time. Weekly jobless claims come at 8:30 a.m. Nebius, another debt-funded cloud builder, reports before the open, and Cisco after the close. Friday brings October producer prices, which will show how much energy and memory-chip costs are still moving through the supply chain. Next Tuesday, Nov. 17, Disney reports in the morning and Nvidia after the close. The Fed meets Dec. 8 and 9.
