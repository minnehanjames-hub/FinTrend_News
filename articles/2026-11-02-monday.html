Monday Outlook · Week of Nov 2

Ballots on Tuesday. Paychecks on Friday.

The S&P 500 starts November 3.1% below its record after Alphabet, Amazon and Intel turned a three-day rally into a weekly loss and the Nasdaq lost 5.3% in October. This week brings Apple tonight, the midterms Tuesday, the Bank of England Thursday and the October jobs report Friday. Iran talks ended without a breakthrough.

James Thomas Minnehan
Senior Editor
MON · NOV 2 · 2026

Last Monday I wrote that the Fed would speak at two and Big Tech would answer at four. The Fed said what it was expected to say. Big Tech answered with a bill. Alphabet had its worst day on record, Intel its worst since 1974, and Amazon disclosed a $42.4 billion cash outflow over the past year. The S&P 500 lost 0.6% on the week to 7,580.07, 3.1% below its Oct. 6 record, and the Nasdaq lost 2.0%. For October, the S&P fell about 0.9% and the Nasdaq 5.3%, while the Dow gained 1.7% and the Russell 2000 held a small gain. The 10-year yield ended at 4.93%, and Brent at $113.43.

This week the market has to absorb three different kinds of news in five days. Apple reports tonight. The country votes on Tuesday, and the result will arrive overnight into Wednesday’s session. The Bank of England decides on Thursday, and the October jobs report lands on Friday morning. Asia started the week on a better note: Seoul’s Kospi rose 2.2% and Shanghai 1.1% after China’s central bank said it would deploy about 100 billion yuan through a new program to support the stock market. European shares opened higher.

1. Tuesday: the midterms

Republicans hold the Senate 53 to 47 and a narrow House majority. Markets tend to have a simple preference in these elections: they like divided government because it lowers the odds of large fiscal or regulatory surprises, and they dislike uncertainty that drags past election night. Beyond that, the channels to watch are the spending bills Congress must pass, trade policy, and oversight of the administration. This year adds two more. The war with Iran and the fuel prices it produced are on voters’ minds, and so are electricity bills, which have turned data center construction into a local campaign issue in several states, most visibly in Texas’s race for governor.

That last point matters for the AI trade in a way it did not two years ago. If the results tilt toward candidates who campaigned on slowing or taxing data center growth, the market will have to price permitting and power risk on top of the funding risk it priced last week. The historical record is supportive: the S&P 500 has risen in the six months after every midterm since 1970. Watch Wednesday’s open, not Tuesday’s close, because most races will be called overnight.

2. Friday: the October jobs report

The September payroll gain was only 29,000, and the October report is the first real test of whether the Fed’s September hike is slowing hiring. Economists surveyed by Dow Jones expect 59,000 jobs, an unemployment rate of 4.2%, and average hourly earnings up 0.3% on the month and 2.9% on the year. The arithmetic for the Fed is simple. A number near consensus keeps a December hike as the base case, which futures priced near 85% after Wednesday’s meeting. A weak number with rising unemployment forces the committee to choose between 3.6% inflation and a softening labor market. A strong one probably settles December.

The rest of the week fills in the picture: the ISM manufacturing index this morning, job openings on Tuesday, the ISM services index on Wednesday, productivity and jobless claims on Thursday, and the University of Michigan’s preliminary sentiment survey on Friday. The price components of both ISM surveys matter as much as the headlines, after September’s PCE report showed energy still feeding into inflation.

3. Earnings: Apple and the memory bill

Apple reports after the close. The iPhone cycle matters less this quarter than the cost side. The same AI data center demand that sent SK Hynix’s profit up more than sevenfold has raised the price of memory chips, and Apple buys enormous quantities of them. If Apple guides its gross margin lower for the holiday quarter, it will be the clearest sign yet that the AI build-out is taxing companies outside the AI supply chain. Palantir also reports tonight, AppLovin on Wednesday, and Axon on Thursday.

The broader season is still strong. Of the 299 S&P 500 companies that had reported through Friday, 82% beat estimates, and LSEG expects third-quarter earnings growth of 31.6%. Most stocks in the index rose on Thursday even as Alphabet fell 12.6%. The weakness is concentrated in the stocks that carry the AI spending plans, and that concentration is why the index can fall while most of its members rise.

Last week the market learned what AI costs. This week it learns what voters think of the power bill, and what the labor market thinks of a 4% funds rate.James Thomas Minnehan

4. Oil and Iran

Iranian negotiators left talks with Qatari mediators on Sunday after several days without a breakthrough. Iran’s foreign minister described the consultations as intensive, and Qatari mediators are expected in Tehran with revised proposals for a draft deal. The Strait of Hormuz remains closed, and a Qatari LNG tanker was struck in the Gulf on Friday. President Trump has repeatedly said there will be no new attack before Tuesday’s vote, which makes Wednesday the first day without that informal guarantee. Brent has traded between roughly $70 and $120 since just before the war began, and it starts this week near the upper end.

5. Central banks beyond the Fed

The Reserve Bank of Australia decides on Tuesday and the Bank of England on Thursday. A Bank of England hike would be its first since 2023 and would make it the latest major central bank, after the Fed in September and the Bank of Canada last week, to tighten into the energy shock. The European Central Bank held last week, and markets are still digesting Christine Lagarde’s plan to step down in February. Fed officials return to the speaking circuit this week after the blackout period, and the message most are likely to repeat is the one from Wednesday’s statement: inflation remains elevated.

The setup in one line

The S&P 500 is 3.1% below its record, the Nasdaq 7.8%, the 10-year below 5% and Brent above $113. The bullish path is a clean election result that markets read as divided government, an in-line jobs report, Apple margins that hold, and progress in the Iran talks. The bearish path is a result that puts data center permitting in doubt, a hot jobs number that seals December, Apple guiding margins lower on memory costs, and renewed strikes in the Gulf once the vote is over. Watch Wednesday’s open, Friday’s payrolls and Brent.
