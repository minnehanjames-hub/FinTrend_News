Friday Recap · Week of Nov 2

The Data Center Went on the Ballot. The AI Trade Paid the Bill.

Democrats won Congress and Texas elected a governor who campaigned against data center projects. The Dow rallied on Wednesday; on Thursday the S&P 500 fell 4.4% as AI stocks sank. A 165,000-job report steadied Friday, but the S&P lost 2.7% on the week, its worst since April 2025.

HWL
Hunter William Lang
Markets Editor
FRI · NOV 6 · 2026

The week split into two markets on Wednesday and collapsed into one on Thursday. Democrats won control of Congress, oil fell below $95, and the Dow rose 3.0% in its best session in months. Twenty-four hours later the S&P 500 fell 4.4%, its worst day since the “Liberation Day” tariff selloff of April 2025, as investors decided the election had put a political ceiling on AI data center construction. Nvidia fell 9.3% and AMD 17.3%. On Friday a jobs report that beat every forecast steadied things: payrolls rose 165,000 and unemployment fell to 4.1%. The S&P 500 rose 0.3% on the day but lost 2.7% for the week to 7,376.86, its worst week since April 2025, and sits 5.7% below its record. The Nasdaq lost 3.4%.

Wednesday: the election rally

By Wednesday morning Democrats had won both chambers of Congress, flipping at least seven Senate seats and roughly 19 in the House on the early count, and in Texas Democrat Gina Hinojosa unseated Gov. Greg Abbott. With President Trump keeping his veto, markets read the result as divided government, and the bigger move came in oil after Vice President JD Vance tied part of the Republican losses to the war with Iran. Brent fell 8.7% to $94.62. The Dow gained 1,569.40 points and the S&P 500 1.6%. Defense contractors and oil producers fell; hospitals, discount retailers, airlines and solar companies rose. The AI names did not join in: Nvidia fell 3.1% and CoreWeave 9.9%.

Thursday: the data center sells off

Thursday showed why. The S&P 500 fell 334.89 points, or 4.4%, to 7,353.73, and the Nasdaq 5.5% to 24,315.00, leaving it 11.9% below its October record and in correction territory. Reuters reported that the Nasdaq 100 was on track to lose more than $2 trillion of market value and that the semiconductor index fell as much as 11.2% intraday. Nvidia dropped 9.3%, AMD 17.3%, CoreWeave 9.8% and power-equipment maker GE Vernova 7.5%. Nvidia is now down 2.6% for the year after three years of large gains. The Dow fell 1,833 points and the Russell 2000 4.4%, so this was not only a tech story.

The trigger was the election map. Texas accounts for about 27% of planned U.S. data center capacity, according to Wells Fargo estimates cited by CNBC, and Morgan Stanley analysts called Hinojosa’s win the most consequential result of the night for data center development. Of the governor races called by the AP, 32 of 34 went to candidates who backed tighter rules on data centers, according to Data Center Watch, and restrictions passed in 44 of 45 completed local referendums across 12 states, according to Redbud Consulting. Voters in one Ohio county approved a ban on large facilities where Meta had planned one. In Washington, Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez have proposed a national moratorium, which would still need the president’s signature. UBS estimated that about 30% of expected U.S. data center capacity additions in 2028 could be at risk from construction pauses.

Financing made it worse. Smaller cloud operators that borrow to build capacity were hit hardest, and five-year credit default swap spreads on CoreWeave widened to a record, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence. Nomura’s Charlie McElligott attributed much of the selling to hedge funds locking in gains before year-end, and Vanda Research said individual investors were net sellers of about $358 million, the most since March 2020. Ted Parkhill of Incline Investment Management put the shift in one line: “The market’s moving from the promise of AI to the price of AI.”

For two weeks the AI trade was judged on cash flow. On Thursday it was judged on permits, power bills and county boards, and that test is harder to pass with a strong quarter.Hunter William Lang

A few stocks moved the other way on results. AppLovin jumped 31% after beating forecasts and approving up to $3.2 billion of additional buybacks, and Tapestry rose 7.1% after raising its outlook. Treasurys rallied as a haven, and the 10-year yield fell to 4.84%. Brent rebounded 3.9% to $98.29. Abroad, Seoul’s Kospi fell 5.3%, and the Bank of England raised its rate a quarter point to 4.00%, its first increase since 2023, on an 8–1 vote.

Friday: the jobs report steadies the floor

Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, far above the roughly 59,000 economists expected, and revisions added 84,000 to August and September combined. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% from 4.2%. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month and 3.1% on the year, both above forecasts. Professional and business services added 70,000 jobs, health care 42,000, transportation and warehousing 25,000 and manufacturing 19,000. After the report, CME’s FedWatch put the odds of a December hike at about 77%. The University of Michigan’s preliminary survey showed consumer sentiment improving unexpectedly and inflation expectations for the year ahead easing.

AI stocks recovered part of Thursday’s loss. Nvidia rose 2.8%, IREN 20% on strong results and new contracts, Lam Research 8.2% after announcing a buyback of up to $25 billion, CoreWeave 6.3% and Micron 5.1%. Axon fell 18.2% after disappointing results, and Charter Communications 6.2% on an unexpected loss. Outside the AI names the market was mixed, and the Dow slipped 0.5%. Tokyo and Seoul each fell about 6% overnight as Thursday’s U.S. selloff reached Asia, and Germany’s DAX lost 1.9%. The 10-year ended at 4.86%.

Oil: the biggest weekly drop since 2020

Brent finished the week just under $98, down roughly 13%, its steepest weekly decline since March 2020, according to Trading Economics. President Trump discussed de-escalation with Qatar’s emir, and Tehran was reported to be reviewing mediators’ proposals, although Iran wants the U.S. naval blockade lifted, sanctions eased and frozen assets released before it fully reopens the Strait of Hormuz. Attacks on ships in the Gulf continue. Cheaper oil is the one piece of this week that helps both inflation and household budgets.

The bottom line

The S&P 500 lost 2.7% this week and is 5.7% below its record; the Nasdaq is in correction territory, about 11% off its high. The economy, on Friday’s evidence, is fine: hiring re-accelerated, wages are rising and unemployment fell. What changed is the political and financial runway for the AI build-out, which has carried this market for three years. Next week brings October CPI on Tuesday and producer prices on Friday, with Brent below $100 and a December hike priced near 77%. The question is whether falling oil can cool inflation fast enough to give the Fed a reason to wait, and whether the AI selloff has priced the new politics or only started to.

The bottom line

Democrats won Congress and Texas elected a governor who campaigned against data center projects.
