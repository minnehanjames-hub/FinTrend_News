Momentum Check: The Fed Held. Then Alphabet Spoke.

A third straight gain left the S&P 500 1% below its record as the Fed held with two votes for a hike and December odds rose to 85%. After the bell, Microsoft rose about 4% on 48% Azure growth while Alphabet fell more than 10% on a revenue miss and a bigger spending bill for 2027.

HWL
Hunter William Lang
Markets Editor
WED · OCT 28 · 2026

Three days, three gains, and a Fed meeting that changed very little on the surface. The S&P 500 rose 0.3% on Wednesday to 7,743.98, up 1.6% for the week and 1.0% below its Oct. 6 record of 7,818.93. The Nasdaq added 0.3% and is up 2.3% since Friday; the Russell 2000 is up 2.0%. The Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.75% to 4.00%, as expected, but two officials voted to raise it, and futures now put the odds of a December hike at 85%, up from 78% on Tuesday. Then, a few minutes after the close, the two companies the market had waited all week for gave opposite answers. Microsoft rose about 4% in extended trading. Alphabet fell more than 10%.

Monday’s spark came from outside the index. Anthropic made its IPO prospectus public, showing first-half revenue of about $16.3 billion, roughly 14 times the level a year earlier, and a net loss of $80.7 billion that was mostly non-cash. It intends to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. After a week in which Meta’s cash burn made investors ask who would pay for all the computing capacity being built, a fast-growing buyer of that capacity was the right headline at the right moment. The Philadelphia semiconductor index rose 2.8% on Monday, Lumentum about 8% and Coherent about 6%. Meta itself slipped 1.9% after saying it plans to raise up to $95 billion in equity for AI infrastructure.

Tuesday was the second leg. The Nasdaq rose 1.2% as chip stocks extended their gains. Oil helped: Brent fell 1.7% to $113.88 and U.S. crude settled just under $100 as Qatari mediators worked on de-escalation with Iran. The Dow barely moved, which tells you where the buying was. This was a tech rebound, not a broad one, although small caps kept rising alongside it.

The Fed: a hold that leans toward December

The decision itself was never in doubt. What mattered was the tone, and the tone leaned hawkish. The statement upgraded the Fed’s view of the economy and said inflation “remains elevated.” Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack both voted for a quarter-point hike; for Cook it was a first dissent. The committee framed the pause as time to gather more information on the way back to 2%, which most strategists read as a December hike that is likely but not promised.

In his press conference, Chair Kevin Warsh said politics played no part in the decision and rejected the idea of a soft inflation target. He said there is broad agreement on the committee that tighter policy is starting to bite, with credit conditions firming for some businesses and households, and he argued that higher rates can cool borrowing for data centers. That last point matters for this market: the Fed chair is telling investors that the AI build-out is not beyond the reach of monetary policy. The Fed also said it will hold fewer policy meetings starting in 2027, without giving details.

Stocks swung during the press conference but finished higher. Ryan Detrick of Carson Group told Reuters the Fed offered no surprises and that investors are getting used to Warsh’s style. The bond market did more of the moving: the 10-year yield climbed back to about 5.05%, after ending last week at 4.99%. North of the border, the Bank of Canada raised its rate a quarter point to 2.5% this week.

Chips did the lifting

Wednesday’s leaders sat in the supply chain. Micron rose 5.9% and SK Hynix’s U.S.-listed shares 13.3% after the Korean memory maker reported a more than sevenfold jump in quarterly profit. Vertiv, which sells power and cooling equipment to data centers, jumped 16.5% after beating forecasts and raising its full-year outlook. The semiconductor index rose 1.8%, its fourth straight gain. On the other side, Boston Scientific fell 5.8% after trimming its profit forecast, and Robinhood lost 3.3% as softer crypto trading pulled its quarterly profit below estimates.

Earnings remain the market’s floor. Of the roughly 200 S&P 500 companies that have reported, 84% beat estimates, according to LSEG, which now puts third-quarter earnings growth at 31.8%, up from 30.6% at the start of the month. Breadth on Wednesday was thin, with advancers barely ahead of decliners on the NYSE, but the index-level trend is intact.

After the bell: same night, opposite verdicts

Microsoft reported revenue of $92.28 billion for its September quarter, up 19% and above the $90.69 billion analysts expected. Azure grew 48%, faster than the 42% of the prior quarter, and for the first time Microsoft disclosed Azure revenue in dollars: $33.17 billion. Capital spending, including finance leases, was $45.2 billion, well below the roughly $50.7 billion analysts had pencilled in, because third-party data center capacity arrived later than planned. Free cash flow fell 27% to $18.63 billion. The stock rose about 4% after hours.

Alphabet reported revenue of $127.58 billion, up 25% but just short of the $127.97 billion FactSet consensus. Earnings were $2.12 a share against roughly $3.01 expected, held down by an $8.7 billion loss on equity investments. Google Cloud revenue more than doubled, up 103% to $30.7 billion. But search and other advertising grew 12% to $63.3 billion, YouTube ads 9%, and the finance chief said capital spending will increase significantly in 2027. The shares fell more than 10% in extended trading, to about $290.

Microsoft spent less than planned and the stock rose. Alphabet promised to spend more and the stock fell. That is the market’s scorecard now.Hunter William Lang

Put the two side by side and the grading system is clear. Cloud growth was not the problem; Alphabet’s cloud business doubled. The market rewarded the company whose spending came in under plan and whose core franchise accelerated, and it punished the one whose ad growth slowed while it promised a bigger bill next year. After Meta last week, that is two of the four biggest spenders judged on cash, not on demand.

Oil: diplomacy against the Houthis

Brent fell on Monday and Tuesday as reports of Qatari mediation in Tehran and President Trump’s weekend comments eased fears of new strikes on Iran. It turned higher on Wednesday, settling up about 1% at $115.32, after the Houthis claimed a missile strike on Saudi Arabia’s largest refinery, a plant that processes about 550,000 barrels a day. Saudi authorities had not confirmed the attack by the settlement. U.S. crude gained about 3% to $102.78. Oil is still where the inflation risk lives, and it is why the Fed would not close the door on December.

Tomorrow and the rest of the week

Thursday is the heavy day. At 8:30 a.m. come the first estimate of third-quarter GDP, which Warsh said likely topped 3%, and September’s PCE inflation, expected at 3.6% from 3.4%, with the core measure seen at 3.1%. The European Central Bank decides later that morning. After the close, Amazon and Intel report. Futures will open Thursday with Alphabet’s drop to absorb, and with communication services a heavy weight in the S&P, the index may start the day on the back foot even if most stocks rise. The Bank of Japan decides overnight, and Friday brings month-end.

The momentum read

The S&P 500 rose for a third straight day to 7,743.98, up 1.6% for the week and 1.0% below its record, as Anthropic’s IPO filing and strong memory-chip earnings revived the AI trade. The Fed held at 3.75% to 4.00% with two votes for a hike, and December odds rose to 85%. After the close, Microsoft rose about 4% on 48% Azure growth and lower-than-expected capex, while Alphabet fell more than 10% on a revenue miss and a warning that spending will rise significantly in 2027. Thursday’s GDP, PCE, ECB, Amazon and Intel decide whether the rebound survives the week.
