The Quiet Number

July closed with the S&P down a tenth of a percent — its first losing July in eleven years — a flat line hiding a violent month. Now a jobs-report week arrives into a Fed that won't ease and an oil market swinging on Iran.

Arshia Morshedian · Macro & Global Markets
Sunday, August 2, 2026

July ended almost exactly where it began. The S&P 500 finished the month down roughly a tenth of a percent — its first losing July in eleven years — a placid, forgettable number that hides one of the most violent months of the year. Underneath that flat line was a war premium in oil, a hawkish Fed hold that broke the bond market to multi-year highs, and four referenda on whether the market would keep paying for the AI buildout. The number was quiet. The month was not.

The month in one flat line

The composure of the monthly return is the story. The Nasdaq fell 3.2% in July as this year's most crowded trade — AI infrastructure — went through a genuine derating, while the Dow rose about 0.3% as money rotated toward the defensive and the cyclical. That dispersion, a tech selloff paired with a blue-chip bid, is what a flat index looks like from the inside. It is also why the last week mattered so much: Microsoft's Azure acceleration and Amazon's AWS reacceleration arrested the derating and reaffirmed the trade, but only after the group had already given back a month of gains.

The Fed did its part to unsettle things. The July hold arrived with three dissents in favor of a hike, the most one-directional dissent since 2016, and no forward guidance to soften it. The bond market took that as a signal the committee may be content to sit still while inflation runs, and it repriced the long end accordingly — the 30-year to levels not seen since 2007. That backdrop, more than any earnings line, is the thing the market carries into August.

A flat monthly return that hides a tech selloff, a blue-chip bid, a hawkish Fed and an oil war premium isn't calm. It's a truce between opposing forces.

Reading the quiet number

The week belongs to Friday

This is a jobs-report week, and the jobs report is the whole week. June's payrolls grew just 57,000, a sharp slowing from the spring, and the consensus for July lands somewhere around 90,000 to 120,000 with the unemployment rate expected to tick up from 4.2% to 4.3%. On its own, a cooling labor market would normally pull rate-cut expectations forward. The complication is the Fed we just watched: Warsh's committee has told the market it will not ease into elevated inflation, which means a soft payroll print does not automatically buy the rate relief a weakening economy would usually earn.

That is the tension to hold in your head all week. A hot number keeps a September hike alive and pressures the high-multiple names that just recovered. A cold number revives recession worry without necessarily delivering the cut that would offset it. The path of least resistance for markets is an in-line print that lets the AI-relief rally breathe — and the labor data has not been obliging lately.

Oil's on-again, off-again war

The weekend brought another turn in the Iran story: the administration says negotiations resume this week, with a framework around the Strait of Hormuz and a denuclearization track to follow, and crude has fallen back below $80 in response. Treat the relief with the skepticism the last two months have earned. This conflict has moved in a repeating rhythm — pause, re-escalation, pause — and each swing has yanked oil ten to twenty percent and, with it, the inflation impulse and the long end of the curve. Until a deal is signed rather than announced, oil remains the single most important variable the Fed can't control and the market can't price.

The earnings baton passes down the cap scale

The mega-cap gauntlet is over; the second wave is more diffuse but no less telling. Palantir reports Monday, AMD Tuesday, and Disney, Uber and Shopify midweek, alongside SpaceX's first quarterly report as a public company. The question that governed the mega-caps — is the spending producing growth, or just spending? — now extends to the software and chip names one rung down. If the AI-capex verdict holds, the beneficiaries broaden. If it wobbles, the relief rally was a week, not a turn.

The setup into August

  • July was flat and fake-calm. A ~0.1% monthly loss masked a 3.2% Nasdaq drop, a defensive Dow bid, a hawkish Fed and an oil war premium — a truce, not a trend.
  • Friday's jobs report is the week. June was a weak +57k; July is seen near 90-120k with unemployment ticking to 4.3%. With a Fed that won't ease into inflation, a soft print cuts both ways.
  • Oil relief, with an asterisk. Iran talks resume and crude is back below $80, but the pause-and-re-escalate pattern means the war premium can snap back overnight.
  • The AI verdict moves down-cap. Palantir, AMD, Disney, Uber, Shopify and a public SpaceX test whether last week's mega-cap relief broadens or fades.
