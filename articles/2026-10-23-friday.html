Friday Recap · Week of Oct 19

The AI Trade Hit a Funding Wall. Oil Hit a Four-Year High.

Three days of gains put the S&P 500 within 0.8% of its record before Meta’s first cash outflow in more than a decade sent the Nasdaq down 2.6% on Thursday. Then Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia pushed Brent above $116. The S&P lost 0.5% on the week; small caps gained 2%.

HWL
Hunter William Lang
Markets Editor
FRI · OCT 23 · 2026

On Wednesday this column said the market had bought the rebound before reading the receipts. On Thursday it read them. By Wednesday’s close the S&P 500 was up 1.3% for the week and 0.8% below its record. Then Meta, which beat on revenue, reported its first cash outflow in more than a decade and set up a new class of non-voting shares that it says could be used to raise capital. The Nasdaq fell 2.6%, its worst day since June, and the S&P 500 had its worst day since July. On Friday, Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia pushed Brent crude to its highest close since mid-2022.

The S&P 500 finished the week down 0.5% at 7,623.68, 2.5% below its Oct. 6 record. The Nasdaq lost 0.7% to 25,955.34. Underneath, the market looked very different. The Dow rose 0.4% and the Russell 2000 gained 2.0%, and on Thursday, the day of the rout, slightly more NYSE stocks rose than fell. This was not a broad sell-off. It was a sell-off in the trade that has carried the index, while the rest of the market and the bond market moved the other way. The 10-year yield ended the week at 4.99%, down from 5.09%, after one of its sharpest one-day drops in years on Thursday.

Thursday: Meta shows what the race costs

Meta’s third quarter was strong on every line except the one that mattered. Revenue rose 26% to $64.60 billion, above the $63.3 billion analysts expected, and the company guided fourth-quarter revenue to $73 billion to $76 billion, a range whose midpoint sits above forecasts. Earnings of $3.83 a share included a $9.89 billion legal charge tied to its settlement with 52 state attorneys general over teen mental health; excluding it, Meta earned $7.40. But capital spending of $34.91 billion exceeded the $32.79 billion of cash its operations generated, leaving free cash flow at negative $2.12 billion. Meta narrowed its 2026 capex range to $135 billion to $145 billion, which implies a heavy fourth quarter, and gave no 2027 forecast.

Then came the share structure. Meta will issue one non-voting Class C share for every Class A and Class B share, effectively a two-for-one split, from Jan. 19, 2027. A special committee of independent directors said the new shares would let the company raise capital, pay employees and fund acquisitions. The stock rose after hours on Wednesday and fell 7.0% on Thursday to $648.85, ending the week down about 9%. Last Monday we wrote that the number to watch was how Meta planned to pay for its spending. Part of the answer, it turns out, is its own stock.

The selling spread fast. The Philadelphia Semiconductor index fell 6.7% to 10,731.98, its biggest drop since June 23, leaving it more than 26% below its June record but still up 51.5% for the year. AMD fell 8.1%, Micron 6.3% and Nvidia 4.4%; Intel, Sandisk and Marvell each lost more than 8%, and CoreWeave about 10%. Tesla fell 3.1% even after posting positive free cash flow and adjusted earnings of $0.48 a share against $0.44 expected, because it cut 2026 capex while flagging heavier spending in 2027 and 2028 on Optimus, Cybercab and Terafab. IBM beat estimates and raised its cash-flow forecast but kept its revenue growth outlook at 4% to 5%, and its shares slid. As Paul Nolte of Murphy & Sylvest put it, investors are starting to see “some holes in the AI story.”

Meta answered the question of how it will pay for AI: partly with its own stock. The market answered back on Thursday.Hunter William Lang

The rest of the market had a good week

The rout was confined to technology. On the same Thursday, Spotify rose 11.1%, Blackstone 8.4% on an upbeat outlook for fourth-quarter asset sales, Lockheed Martin 7.0%, Procter & Gamble 5.6% and Union Pacific 3.6%. Of the 111 S&P 500 companies that had reported by Thursday, 83% beat expectations, and LSEG data show third-quarter earnings on track to grow more than 32%. Weekly jobless claims fell to 194,000, the fewest in three months. The Russell 2000’s 2.0% weekly gain, against the Nasdaq’s 0.7% loss, is the clearest sign that the market is rotating, not breaking.

Friday: oil takes the wheel again

The second shock came from the Gulf. Iran-backed Houthis struck targets in Saudi Arabia, including an airport in Dammam and an Aramco oil field, and on Thursday the New York Times reported that the U.S. and Israel had drawn up plans for renewed strikes on Iran after the Nov. 3 midterms. Brent rose to a five-month high above $113 on Thursday and another 3.1% on Friday to about $116.72, its highest close since mid-2022 and 6.2% higher on the week. U.S. crude settled near $103. The S&P 500’s energy sector rose 2.4% on Friday to a record.

The rest of Friday was defensive. Most sectors fell, led by consumer staples and financials, and NYSE decliners outnumbered advancers by nearly two to one. The Dow lost 652.82 points, or 1.2%. American Express fell 3.9% even after raising its profit forecast, because it also announced plans to cut 5% of its workforce. AT&T and T-Mobile each sank more than 6% and Verizon 1.6% on a report that SpaceX was seeking thousands of cell sites for a fourth wireless carrier, while tower owners Crown Castle and American Tower rose. SLB gained 6.7% on a profit beat. Technology steadied, and the chip index rose 0.6%.

The data: a booming economy with rising prices

S&P Global’s flash composite PMI slipped to 57.6 from 58.4, still the second-highest reading since March 2022, and the manufacturing index rose to 56.0, a 53-month high. The worrying part was prices: selling prices rose at the steepest rate in four years as oil fed through to goods and services. Chris Williamson of S&P Global said the survey points to the economy still booming at the start of the fourth quarter, at roughly a 5% annualized growth rate, and warned that inflation could move further above the Fed’s target. The final University of Michigan survey revised sentiment up to 48.0 from 46.3, still a five-month low. Year-ahead inflation expectations held at 4.7% and the long-run gauge at 3.5%, up from 3.4% in September.

Bonds: a haven, with conditions

The 10-year yield rose to 5.14% on Monday and held at 5.12% through Wednesday, then fell below 5% on Thursday as money left the AI trade, and ended Friday at 4.99%. The auctions told a more cautious story. Wednesday’s $13 billion 20-year sale tailed by 0.9 basis point at 5.469%, and Thursday’s $26 billion five-year TIPS cleared at a real yield of 2.367%, a result TIPSWatch called disappointing even on a strong day for Treasurys. Bloomberg noted that inflation breakevens sit slightly below pre-war levels, which means this year’s rise in yields has come from real rates, not from inflation fears. Bonds work as a hedge when AI wobbles, but new supply still has to be priced to sell.

The bottom line

The S&P is 2.5% below its record, small caps are rising, bonds are rallying and oil is at a four-year high. That is not a market collapsing. It is a market repricing which AI spenders can fund their plans from cash, and how much an oil shock costs. Next week brings both questions to a head. The Fed decides on Wednesday at 2 p.m., with a hold expected and a December hike priced as likely. Microsoft and Alphabet report after the close that day, Amazon and Intel on Thursday, alongside third-quarter GDP and September PCE inflation. Israel votes on Tuesday. Watch whether Microsoft and Alphabet’s operating cash flow still covers their spending, and whether Warsh leans on December.

The bottom line

The S&P 500 fell 0.5% to 7,623.68 and the Nasdaq 0.7% as Meta’s first cash outflow in over a decade and a new non-voting share class reopened the AI funding question, sending the chip index down 6.7% on Thursday. The rest of the market held up: the Russell 2000 rose 2.0%, the Dow 0.4%, and 83% of reporting companies beat estimates. Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia pushed Brent 6.2% higher to about $116.72, its highest close since 2022, while the 10-year fell to 4.99%. Next week: the Fed on Wednesday, then Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon.
